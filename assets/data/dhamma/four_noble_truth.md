# ☸️ The Four Noble Truths (Cattāri Ariyasaccāni)

> "I teach one thing and one thing only: suffering and the cessation of suffering." — *The Buddha*

---

## 📜 Summary of the Truths

| Truth (Pali) | Translation | Essential Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dukkha Sacca** | The Truth of Suffering | To be fully comprehended (*Pariññeyya*) |
| **Samudaya Sacca** | The Truth of the Origin of Suffering | To be abandoned (*Pahātabba*) |
| **Nirodha Sacca** | The Truth of the Cessation of Suffering | To be realized (*Sacchikātabba*) |
| **Magga Sacca** | The Truth of the Path to Cessation | To be cultivated (*Bhāvetabba*) |

---

## 🛣️ The Noble Eightfold Path (*Ariya Aṭṭhaṅgika Magga*)

The practical path leading to the cessation of suffering is grouped into three divisions (*Khandha*):

### 1. Wisdom (*Paññā*)
* **Right View (*Sammā Diṭṭhi*):** Understanding the Four Noble Truths and the nature of impermanence (*Anicca*).
* **Right Intention (*Sammā Saṅkappa*):** Commitment to renunciation, goodwill, and harmlessness.

### 2. Moral Conduct (*Sīla*)
* **Right Speech (*Sammā Vācā*):** Abstaining from lying, divisive speech, harsh words, and idle chatter.
* **Right Action (*Sammā Kammanta*):** Abstaining from harming living beings, stealing, and unwholesome behavior.
* **Right Livelihood (*Sammā Ājīva*):** Engaging in work that avoids causing harm to self or others.

### 3. Mental Discipline (*Samādhi*)
* **Right Effort (*Sammā Vāyāma*):** Preventing unwholesome states of mind and cultivating wholesome ones.
* **Right Mindfulness (*Sammā Sati*):** Maintaining clear awareness of body, feelings, mind, and mental phenomena.
* **Right Concentration (*Sammā Samādhi*):** Developing single-pointed focus and meditative absorption (*Jhāna*).

---

## 🌿 The Three Marks of Existence (*Tilakkhaṇa*)

- **Anicca:** Impermanence of all conditioned phenomena.
- **Dukkha:** Unsatisfactoriness inherent in clinging to what changes.
- **Anattā:** Non-self; the absence of a permanent, unchanging core or essence.
